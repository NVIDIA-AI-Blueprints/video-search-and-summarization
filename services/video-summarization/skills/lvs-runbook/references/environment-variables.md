### Elasticsearch tuning

Each `/v1/summarize` and `/v1/stream/add` creates a fresh `default_<asset_id>` index with `number_of_shards: 1`. Long-running stress workloads previously hit Elasticsearch's default `cluster.max_shards_per_node=1000` cap and silently hung; the bug is mitigated by raising the cap and (in drop-mode) reclaiming shards on completion.

| Variable | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `ES_MAX_SHARDS_PER_NODE` | `2000` | Maps to ES's `cluster.max_shards_per_node`. 2× ES's native 1000 default — modest headroom for retain-mode workloads (`LVS_DISABLE_DB_RESET_ON_REQUEST_DONE=true`). Drop-mode deployments rarely hit even the legacy 1000 cap because completed indices are binned. **Must be tuned together with `ES_JAVA_OPTS` — see below.** |
| `ES_JAVA_OPTS` | `-Xms4g -Xmx4g` | Elasticsearch JVM heap. ES cluster-state size scales with active shards: their guidance is to keep active shards under ~20 per GB of heap. The 4 GB default safely supports ~80 active shards in steady state and a few hundred under brief stress. To support the 2000-shard cap at *sustained retain-mode* operation you need ~100 GB heap (impractical on a single node); the cap is therefore a hard ceiling, not a target. For high-stress retain workloads, raise both knobs together and stay under ~32 GB heap (compressed-oops boundary) — beyond that requires multi-node ES. Drop-mode workloads keep active shards at ~1 regardless of cap, so the 4 GB default is comfortable. |
