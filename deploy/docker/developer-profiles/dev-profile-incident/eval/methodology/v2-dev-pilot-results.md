Reading these (development only):
- **Reliability:** Super and Nano are the only models with near-complete contract validity. Omni, Qwen and Gemma lose 24–52% of their runs to invalid outputs or runaway reasoning.
- **Severity:** on runs where both are valid, Gemma's MAE is lower than Super's, and Qwen's and Omni's point estimates are lower too, though their intervals include zero. Super and Nano rarely pick the exact level.
- **Type and objects:** Super is the strongest. Nano's entity F1 is far below Super's (Nano lists many people the ground truth does not have).
- **Timing:** Gemma's end time is unreliable (§5).

Per clip-length bucket (≤ 30 s, 30–60 s, > 60 s): [`track_a_by_duration.csv`](results/v2-dev/track_a_by_duration.csv). Gemma's validity rises with clip length (0.27, 0.50, 0.75). Qwen's falls (1.00, 0.67, 0.50) as long clips trigger runaway reasoning.

## 5. Provider, video-input and comparability limitations

The same P1 v4 request (prompt, strict `json_schema`, settings) went to every Track A model as an inline base64 MP4. **How each serving stack turns the video into model input differs**, so Track A compares *models as served*, not bare model capability.

| Model | Route and providers | Frames the model receives (probes, 2 Nov) | Temporal information | Decoding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmos 3 Nano | Switchyard | Ignores `num_frames`; samples by clip length | Provider-side; not documented | Strict `json_schema` honoured |
| Cosmos 3 Super | Switchyard | Honours `num_frames` = 64 (about 44 tokens a frame) | Provider-side; not documented | Strict |
| Nemotron 3 Nano Omni | Switchyard | Honours `num_frames` = 64 (about 141 tokens a frame) | Provider-side; not documented | Strict |
| Qwen3.5-9B | OpenRouter → DeepInfra or Parasail | About 1 frame a second by length (self-reported 9 / 26 / 81 frames for 8 / 25 / 80 s); prompt grows about 36 tokens a second | Qwen-VL processors build textual timestamps from the video's fps metadata; predicted end times track real time (median 0.98 × duration) | Strict |
| Gemma 4 31B | OpenRouter → Crusoe or Parasail | Fixed budget: prompt about 6 k tokens for every clip length (vLLM: at most 32 frames × 70 tokens) | vLLM inserts `mm:ss` labels between frames, yet predictions are compressed toward a fixed range (median predicted end 26 s on 13 s clips, 83 s on 107 s clips) | Strict |

- **Gemma's timestamps (offline investigation).** All 26 of Gemma's invalid outputs fail one rule only: a start or end time past the end of the clip. The failure rate is the same on both providers (Crusoe 14/28 invalid, Parasail 12/22), so it is not one host's bug.
  - **What the data shows:** predicted times correlate weakly with true times (r = 0.55) and not at all with a "frame index as seconds" model (r = 0.01).
  - **What the data cannot show:** whether the provider passes wrong timestamps or the model ignores the ones it gets.
  - **Proposed diagnostic (not run):** 3 calls to Crusoe on the 8, 25 and 80 s dev clips, asking for the timestamp labels on the first and last frames and the frame count. At most 3 calls, 0.1 M tokens and $0.01.
  - **For scoring:** Gemma's timing is a provider-sampling limitation. Invalid outputs stay invalid; nothing is repaired.
- **Qwen's provider effect.** All 9 of Qwen's runaway-reasoning failures (16,384 output tokens with no answer) happened on Parasail: 10 of 19 Parasail runs were valid, against 28 of 30 on DeepInfra. For C3 the proposal pins Qwen to DeepInfra and discloses this pilot effect. The choice uses development data only.
- **Not comparable across routes:** frame counts, resolution per frame, precision (bf16 or fp8 by provider), and the absolute latency of Switchyard against public providers.
- **Comparable across routes:** the request, the contract, the validation and the scorer.
